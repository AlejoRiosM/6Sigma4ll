# 6Sigma4ll - Six Sigma Made Simple

Welcome to this repository about **Six Sigma**.

This project is designed to explain Six Sigma in easy words for people who are new to the topic.  
The goal is to make the ideas practical, clear, and simple to understand.

Six Sigma is a method for improving processes, reducing mistakes, and making work more consistent.  
It helps teams understand why problems happen, use data to find the real cause, and create better results.

---

## What is Six Sigma?

Six Sigma is a structured way to improve a process.

In simple terms, it helps answer questions like:

- Why are errors happening?
- Where is the process failing?
- What causes delays or defects?
- How can we make the process better and more stable?

Six Sigma does not rely only on opinions.  
It uses data, analysis, and clear steps to make decisions.

---

## Why Six Sigma matters

Six Sigma is important because it helps organizations:

- Reduce defects and errors.
- Improve quality.
- Save time and money.
- Make processes more predictable.
- Increase customer satisfaction.
- Support better decision-making.

The main idea is to improve work using facts instead of guesses.

---

## Six Sigma history

Six Sigma is connected to the history of statistical process control and quality improvement.

Over time, ideas from statistics, process control, Lean thinking, and continuous improvement helped shape the Six Sigma method.  
The approach became widely known because it gave organizations a practical way to improve results using structured problem-solving and data.

## Belts and roles

Six Sigma uses belt colors to show different levels of knowledge and responsibility.

### White Belt
A White Belt has a basic introduction to Six Sigma.  
This level is useful for people who need to understand the language and purpose of the method.

### Yellow Belt
A Yellow Belt understands the basic concepts of Six Sigma and supports improvement work.  
This level often includes simple tools, data collection, and an introduction to hypothesis testing.

### Green Belt
A Green Belt works more directly with improvement projects.  
Green Belts usually support data analysis, process improvement, and project activities under the guidance of a Black Belt or Master Black Belt.

### Black Belt
A Black Belt usually leads improvement projects.  
This role requires stronger knowledge of Six Sigma tools, statistics, project management, and process improvement.

### Master Black Belt
A Master Black Belt is the highest technical level.  
This person coaches Black Belts and Green Belts, supports complex problems, and helps guide the overall Six Sigma effort.

### Champion / Sponsor
A Champion or Sponsor is not usually a belt, but the role is very important.  
This person supports the project, removes obstacles, provides resources, and helps make sure the work stays aligned with business goals.

## DMAIC and DMADV

Six Sigma uses two main roadmaps.

### DMAIC
DMAIC is used when a process already exists and needs improvement.

It stands for:

- Define.
- Measure.
- Analyze.
- Improve.
- Control.

This roadmap helps teams understand the problem, study the current process, find root causes, test solutions, and keep the improvement in place.

### DMADV
DMADV is used when a new process, product, or service needs to be designed.

It stands for:

- Define.
- Measure.
- Analyze.
- Design.
- Verify.

This roadmap is useful when improving the current process is not enough and a new design is needed.

## How projects are selected

Not every problem should become a Six Sigma project.

A good project usually has:

- A clear problem.
- Available data.
- A stable process.
- A realistic timeline.
- A likely solution.
- Support from leadership.
- A benefit for the customer or the business.

Six Sigma teams often prioritize projects based on impact, urgency, cost, and whether the problem can really be solved with the method.

---

## Team structure

A Six Sigma project usually works best with a small, focused team.

Common roles include:

- Project leader.
- Process owner.
- Process expert.
- Team members.
- Subject matter experts.
- Sponsor or Champion.

Some people join the project full time, while others help only when needed.  
The goal is to build a team with the right knowledge, resources, and support.

---

## Basic project flow

A typical Six Sigma project follows this path:

1. Identify the problem.
2. Define the goal.
3. Gather data.
4. Study the process.
5. Find the root cause.
6. Test solutions.
7. Put controls in place.
8. Measure the results.

This makes the work structured and repeatable.

## Hypothesis testing roadmap

Hypothesis testing is a way to check whether a result is real or just random.

It is often used during the Analyze phase of DMAIC.

### 1. Define the question
Example: Did the new process reduce defects?

### 2. Write the hypotheses
- Null hypothesis: there is no meaningful difference.
- Alternative hypothesis: there is a meaningful difference.

### 3. Choose a significance level
A common choice is 0.05.

### 4. Collect the data
Use clean, relevant data from the process.

### 5. Choose the right test
The test depends on the type of data and the question.

Examples:
- t-test
- chi-square test
- ANOVA
- proportion test

### 6. Run the test
Compare the data and calculate the result.

### 7. Interpret the result
Decide whether the evidence supports a real change.

### 8. Explain it simply
Translate the statistical result into business language.

### 9. Connect it to action
Use the result to support improvement decisions.
